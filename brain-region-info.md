**Prefrontal Cortex (PFC)**
- **Cognitive/Executive Functions**
    - Functions: Sustaining attention, inhibiting distractions, inhibiting impulses, working memory, cognitive flexibility (set-shifting), planning, organizing, prioritizing tasks, decision-making, problem-solving, goal-directed behavior, initiating actions, sequencing and ordering information, abstract reasoning, error detection and correction, adapting behavior to changing rules, managing multitasking, maintaining focus over time (vigilance), suppressing irrelevant thoughts and memories.
    - Explanation: Often underactive or inefficiently connected in ADHD; relies on optimal dopamine and norepinephrine levels. Disruption leads to distractibility, forgetfulness, impulsivity, and poor planning.
- **Emotional/Social Functions**
    - Functions: Regulating emotions, empathy and perspective-taking, social judgment, moral reasoning, regulating aggression and socially inappropriate behavior, reward evaluation and delayed gratification.
    - Explanation: The PFC's role in emotional regulation is critical; when impaired in ADHD, it contributes to emotional dysregulation and impulsivity often seen alongside core symptoms.
- **Motor Functions**
    - Functions: Motor planning and control of voluntary movement.
    - Explanation: The PFC contributes to the planning phase of movement, working in concert with motor cortices and subcortical structures.
- **Integrative/Language Functions**
    - Functions: Language processing (e.g., verbal fluency, semantic processing), integrating information from other brain regions.
    - Explanation: The PFC acts as a hub, coordinating inputs from multiple brain areas to support complex cognition and behavior.

**Striatum (Basal Ganglia)**
- **Action Selection and Inhibition**
    - Functions: Selecting and executing appropriate actions, suppressing inappropriate actions, behavioral inhibition.
    - Explanation: Part of the frontostriatal network; structural and functional differences (especially in the caudate nucleus) contribute to problems with inhibition in ADHD.
- **Learning and Memory**
    - Functions: Procedural learning, habit formation, reward-based learning, building higher-order representations of sequential actions.
    - Explanation: The striatum generates patterns for movement and cognitive processes, learning and laying down memories important for guiding actions. Repeated firing leads to habit development, modulated by reward-related dopamine signals.
- **Cognitive Functions**
    - Functions: Working memory, cognitive flexibility (set-shifting).
    - Explanation: The basal ganglia participate in circuits involving cortex and thalamus that support motor control, reward processing, and procedural learning.

**Cerebellum**
- **Motor Control and Coordination**
    - Functions: Coordinating balance, eye movements, muscle tone, limb movement; planning coordinated, properly timed movement sequences; comparing intended movement with actual movement and making adjustments; coordinating rapid coordinated movements.
    - Explanation: The cerebellum constantly compares intended movement with actual movement and makes appropriate adjustments. It coordinates motor acts so they happen in harmony, without shocks.
- **Motor Learning**
    - Functions: Motor learning, memory storage for motor tasks.
    - Explanation: The cerebellum participates actively in learning processes of motor tasks and memory storage.
- **Non-Motor (Cognitive/Emotional) Functions**
    - Functions: Perceptual, cognitive, emotional processes; verbal fluency; working memory; spatial tasks; executive tasks (random number generation, complex decision-making); affective/emotional processing; language.
    - Explanation: The neocerebellum is linked to non-sensorimotor functions including perceptual, cognitive, and emotional processes. Its role in nonmotor cognitive and affective functions is increasingly recognized.

**Corpus Callosum**
- **Interhemispheric Integration**
    - Functions: Connecting the brain's two hemispheres, bilateral integration of sensory, motor, or higher cognitive functions, interhemispheric transfer of information.
    - Explanation: The corpus callosum is crucial for bilateral integration of sensory, motor, or higher cognitive functions. Research on split-brain patients demonstrated its role in interhemispheric transfer of visual information.
- **Functional Lateralization**
    - Functions: Supporting hemispheric specialization, inhibiting and exciting contralateral hemispheres, interhemispheric inhibition.
    - Explanation: The corpus callosum can have both inhibitory and excitatory effects on the contralateral hemisphere. Its microstructure predicts interhemispheric inhibition function.
- **Metacontrol**
    - Functions: Interhemispheric integration, combining different types of processing in the two hemispheres to yield a single behavioral response.
    - Explanation: This phenomenon is linked to how different types of processing in the two hemispheres are combined to yield a single behavioral response.

**Limbic System (Amygdala and related structures)**
- **Emotion Processing**
    - Functions: Assigning emotional significance or value to sensory information, fear-related responses, emotional behavior, feelings of pleasure.
    - Explanation: The amygdala is an essential component of the circuitry underlying fear-related responses. It plays a key role in emotional behavior, linked to both fear responses and feelings of pleasure.
- **Memory and Learning**
    - Functions: Forming and storing memories for emotive events, emotional learning, fear conditioning.
    - Explanation: The amygdala is involved in forming and storing memories for emotive events. Long-term synaptic plasticity of inputs to the amygdala underlies acquisition of fear memory.
- **Survival Behaviors**
    - Functions: Feeding, mating, emotion, homeostatic responses, fundamental survival behaviors.
    - Explanation: The limbic system is implicated in higher integration of visceral, olfactory, and somatic information as well as homeostatic responses including fundamental survival behaviors.
- **Social and Autonomic Functions**
    - Functions: Emotional and behavioral responses, social communication processing, autonomic responses.
    - Explanation: Cells responding to complex aspects of social communications are found in the amygdala. The limbic system coordinates visceral reactions with emotion and motivation.

**Overall Framework**
- Functions: Integrated attention, self-regulation, and motor control.
- Explanation: ADHD is a network disorder — symptoms arise from disrupted circuits connecting these regions, not from a single area failing.
